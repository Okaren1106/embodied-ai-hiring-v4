The independent script is `work/empirical/verify_paired_independent.py`; results are `analysis/empirical/paired_independent_verification.json`.

**Numerical conclusion:** all 11,083 identity and arithmetic comparisons passed. The independently rebuilt estimates include the three weighting rules, explicit source joins, leave-one-firm-out values, all 128 semantic readings and their envelopes. This verifies computation, not the correctness of the original source coding.

## What the comparison establishes

The two target measurements refer to the same advertisements within each task. Consequently an advertisement's company, wave, location, advertised seniority and full text are identical across its two target rows. The difference is not generated by selecting one set of job advertisements for Python and another set for PyTorch. The common-mention subgroup strengthens this point by requiring both names in the same source advertisement: a positive direct-expression difference there cannot arise merely because one technology name is absent.

This is a descriptive comparison of how selected technologies are articulated. It does not identify a technology effect, control for technology-specific clauses, or equalize the subtasks, candidate attributes and linguistic operators associated with the two names. A programming language and a framework are different kinds of objects; Altium is one named application while EMC/SI/PI is a family of methods. Neither is a randomized or exchangeable treatment.

The all-advertisement difference is algebraically identical to subtracting the two marginal rates computed on the same states. The v4 P-to-U correction changes the robot-model value relative to frozen v3 Table I; it does not change this identity. Reformatting rows into pairs therefore does not itself create a new estimator or new evidence. The additional empirical content is the joint-state structure, the restriction to advertisements that mention both names, and the sensitivity of that within-advertisement pattern to firm weights, exact repeated qualification text, semantic readings and omitted firms. The revised manuscript should make that substantive extension explicit instead of claiming that pairing alone resolves measurement identification.

## Unknown assignments

For each advertisement the difference interval `[1(Qa=P)−1(Qb≠Z), 1(Qa≠Z)−1(Qb=P)]` is correct for unrestricted binary assignments to the two unresolved Q states. Positive weights preserve the aggregate outer interval. The seven recorded semantic switches are coupled as previously specified, including the Python/PyTorch stack, and the code applies them jointly before recalculating bounds.

If unresolved occurrences share a clause or operator, actual source-consistent resolutions may be linked. Unrestricted U assignments can include combinations that are not jointly defensible. These bounds remain conservative outer assignment ranges; do not call them sharp identified sets without explicitly establishing joint feasibility. They are not confidence intervals, are not probabilities over readings, and do not cover errors in resolved P/Z states, omitted technologies or source omissions.

## Common-mention selection

The subgroup is an explicitly selected estimand, not a matched population. It changes the model-task sample from 105 advertisements at 22 firms to 66 at 18; runtime changes from 87 at 20 to 48 at 14; board work from 56 at 18 to 17 at nine. Text length, writing style and the task bundle may affect whether both technologies are mentioned. The subgroup supports “among advertisements naming both technologies,” not an adjusted industry demand contrast. The two mention rates are mechanically 100% in this subgroup, so a mention-rate difference of zero is a construction fact rather than a result.

Retain all-advertisement estimates as the primary result and common-mention estimates as the diagnostic. A same-source comparison removes between-sample advertisement composition; conditioning on both mentions does not identify how the omitted advertisements would have been written.

## Firm and template weighting

Firm-equal weighting answers an average across the observed firms, distinct from the share across observed advertisements. It is not inherently the better estimate and it gives small cells greater per-advertisement weight. Subgroup firm weighting is recomputed over firms actually present in that subgroup; it therefore does not retain the all-advertisement firm composition.

The template rule is Unicode NFKC, case and whitespace normalization, followed by exact equality of the requirements field, with full-description fallback. The code correctly equalizes these text clusters within firms and then equalizes firms. It does not cluster templates across firms, near-duplicate qualification text, or common individual clauses. Comparing this scheme with firm-equal weighting isolates the effect of this exact repetition rule; comparing it directly with advertisement weighting also changes firm weights.

There are 99 exact qualification-text clusters among 105 robot-model advertisements, and all 87 runtime and 56 board advertisements have distinct normalized qualification text under this rule. Template weighting is therefore identical to firm-equal weighting in the latter two tasks. Describe this as a limited exact-text repetition diagnostic, not general control for templates or writing style.

## Required presentation corrections

1. The original output label `known_task_support` is too strong. The code removes four advertisements flagged in a diagnostic of 105 pairs from 94 advertisements. The remaining advertisements were not all independently confirmed in that diagnostic. Use `excluding_flagged_task_boundaries` or equivalent. Historical author review can be described separately.
2. Replace “whitespace-normalized” alone with “Unicode-, case- and whitespace-normalized exact qualification text.” The implementation makes all three transformations.
3. Do not turn the 22-firm coverage of the model-task average into 22 independent replications of the positive direction. Firm-specific intervals and signs can differ. A pooled leave-one-firm-out result establishes that no single firm is necessary for that particular pooled direction; it does not establish universality.
4. The v4 primary common-mention runtime upper endpoint is −4.17 percentage points in advertisement weighting. Its direction survives recorded unknown assignments and semantic switches, but the endpoint remains sensitive to additional classification error. Keep it secondary to the larger model-task contrast rather than describing all task-pair patterns as equally robust.
5. The board common-mention estimate is exactly zero under primary advertisement weighting and negative under firm weighting. This is an informative weighting dependence; do not selectively report only the negative comparison.

## Suggested interpretation

“In this corpus, technologies named together in the same robot-learning advertisement are not equally likely to be expressed as direct candidate qualifications. Python is mentioned less often than PyTorch across all robot-learning advertisements, but its direct-expression share is higher even under the recorded unknown and semantic alternatives. The pattern persists when both technologies are mentioned and under alternative weighting of the observed firms. It characterizes recruitment wording for the selected targets; it does not establish relative technology demand, skill scarcity or a technology-specific causal effect.”

Any use of “direct candidate qualifications” in that formulation must now specify **unqualified target-specific expression**. The subsequent qualification-scope audit found that Python does not lead in broad GENERAL/REQUIRED context, and example expansion changes the direction. See `qualification_scope_conceptual_audit.md` and `local_template_sensitivity.md`. The separate classification-breakdown analysis shows why the small-cell cross-firm reversals should remain illustrations.
