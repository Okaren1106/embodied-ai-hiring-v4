## Fixed question and evidence

Python is a language and PyTorch a framework; their conventional placement in qualification lists may differ. The control compares two frameworks in the same advertisements to see whether the cross-category ranking change recurs. Its success does not require another reversal.

The separate diagnostic adds equality only for N0149 in Q/Q+E/Q+C+E and N0798 in Q. It imposes no equality on arbitrary co-occurrence or other U records.

## Contribution and remaining limits

This informative non-reversal limits the breadth of the original claim: Python–PyTorch ranking change does not recur in this same-class pair. Targets sharing one relation expression move together when the construct changes. The evidence supports a context- and pair-specific measurement result, not a general law of skill rankings.

It does not establish technology ontology as a causal mechanism. Selection was after discovery; only26 advertisements co-mention the targets with complete nesting; companies, texts and conventions overlap with the original analysis. The 等 rule, preferred boundary and captured-source limits remain. There are no application, hiring, wage or skill-use outcomes, and no fabricated human benchmark.

Relative to GOJA this is not new relation extraction or strength annotation. Relative to Daly et al. and Kalyani et al. it does not connect advertisements to worker skill use or externally validated technology adoption. The defensible addition is a reproducible account of how explicit constructs and recorded relation uncertainty alter fixed-denominator comparisons, accompanied by a same-class case with no reversal.

The largest remaining empirical threat is that company list-writing conventions and treatment of 等/open lists generate the cross-category pattern. The complete44-increment source review and admission alternatives expose this threat rather than eliminate it. The control improves the scope of the claim; more weighting tables cannot substitute for external semantic or behavioral validity.
